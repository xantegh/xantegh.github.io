Land Mobile Radio

Digital Land Mobile Radio Systems​

All three — DMR, NXDN, and P25 — are digital voice and data communication standards used in professional radio systems (public safety, utilities, industry, and amateur radio).
They share the same goal: replacing analog FM systems with digital modulation to achieve better spectrum efficiency, voice quality, encryption, and interoperability.

DMR (Digital Mobile Radio)​

Standard: ETSI TS 102 361
Bandwidth: 12.5 kHz
Access: 2-slot TDMA
Modulation: 4FSK (2 bits/symbol)
Voice Codec: AMBE+2

How DMR Works​

DMR uses Time Division Multiple Access (TDMA) to divide a 12.5 kHz channel into two time slots, effectively allowing two simultaneous voice or data paths on a single frequency pair.
Each slot carries digital voice or data, alternating every 30 ms. Radios synchronize using a color code, similar to CTCSS/DCS in analog systems.

Highlights​

  • Tier I: License-free (PMR446, Europe)
  • Tier II: Conventional licensed
  • Tier III: Trunked systems
  • Supports short data messages, telemetry, and GPS
  • Encryption: Basic (ARC4) or AES

Common Uses​

Commercial, utilities, transportation, schools, and ham radio (e.g., BrandMeister DMR network).
Manufacturers: Motorola (MOTOTRBO), Hytera, Tait, Simoco.

Pros​

  • Two voice paths per 12.5 kHz (very efficient)
  • Widely available and multi-vendor
  • Supports roaming, data, text, and IP linking

Cons​

  • Requires GPS-locked repeaters for TDMA synchronization
  • Some proprietary extensions between vendors

NXDN​

Standard: Developed by Icom and Kenwood (open standard via NXDN Forum)
Bandwidth: 12.5 kHz or 6.25 kHz
Access: FDMA
Modulation: 4FSK
Voice Codec: AMBE+2

How NXDN Works​

NXDN uses Frequency Division Multiple Access (FDMA) — each user occupies their own 6.25 kHz or 12.5 kHz channel.
This achieves high spectral efficiency, allowing twice as many channels in the same spectrum compared to analog.

Highlights​

  • Available in conventional or trunked forms (Type-C and Type-D)
  • Supports digital voice and short data messaging
  • Optional AES/DES encryption
  • IP linking for wide-area networking

Common Uses​

Railroads, industrial, utilities, and local public safety agencies.
Manufacturers: Icom (IDAS), Kenwood (NEXEDGE).

Pros​

  • True 6.25 kHz channel spacing (high spectral efficiency)
  • Simpler infrastructure than TDMA
  • Good range and audio quality

Cons​

  • No dual-slot capability
  • Limited interoperability outside Kenwood/Icom ecosystem

Project 25 (P25)​

Standard: TIA-102 series
Bandwidth: 12.5 kHz (Phase 1) / 6.25 kHz equivalent (Phase 2)
Access: FDMA (Phase 1), TDMA (Phase 2)
Modulation: C4FM (Phase 1), H-CPM (Phase 2)
Voice Codec: IMBE (Phase 1), AMBE+2 (Phase 2)

How P25 Works​

P25 was designed primarily for public safety and interoperability between agencies.

  • Phase 1 (FDMA): One digital voice channel. Uses C4FM modulation; true 12.5 kHz occupied bandwidth.
  • Phase 2 (TDMA): two simultaneous voice channels, Uses H-CPM / H-DQPSK modulation; still occupies 12.5 kHz RF bandwidth, but 6.25 kHz spectral efficiency per voice path.

Highlights​

  • Operates in trunked and conventional modes
  • Standardized signaling (CAI, ISSI, CSSI) for multi-vendor compatibility
  • Native AES encryption and Over-the-Air Rekeying (OTAR)
  • Backward-compatible with analog FM

Common Uses​

Used by police, fire, EMS, and federal agencies in North America.
Manufacturers: Motorola, Harris, EF Johnson, Tait, BK Technologies.

Pros​

  • High interoperability and standardization
  • Designed for mission-critical operations
  • Full encryption and data support

Cons​

  • Expensive equipment and infrastructure
  • Complex network management
  • Proprietary vendor extensions (e.g., Motorola ASTRO 25)

System Fusion (C4FM)​

Developed by: Yaesu
Bandwidth: 12.5 kHz
Access: FDMA
Modulation: C4FM
Codec: AMBE+2

How System Fusion Works​

System Fusion (marketed as Yaesu Fusion) is a digital and analog-compatible system designed for amateur radio. It supports C4FM digital voice and automatic mode switching between analog FM and digital.

Highlights​

  • Dual-mode (analog + digital)
  • Clear, natural-sounding digital audio
  • Uses Wires-X network for linking repeaters globally

Pros​

  • Easy transition from analog FM
  • Excellent audio quality
  • Good integration with repeater networks

Cons​

  • Proprietary to Yaesu
  • Smaller network footprint than DMR or D-STAR
